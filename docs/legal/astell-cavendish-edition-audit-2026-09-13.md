# Astell and Cavendish edition audit — 13 September 2026

Decision: both exact text bundles pass the project's conservative United States publication screen. Eight passages per book have been selected and source-verified. This is an assistant review of the identified sources, not a claim of worldwide legal clearance or human legal review. The reviewed publication package adds only the approved exact sources.

## Mary Astell, A Serious Proposal to the Ladies

- Exact provider edition: [Project Gutenberg 54984](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/54984), downloaded as [pg54984.txt](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/54984/pg54984.txt). The catalogue identifies Mary Astell (1666–1731) and labels this ebook public domain in the USA.
- The complete [transcribed source](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/54984/pg54984-images.html) has a London, Richard Wilkin,1697 title page describing two parts. Part II has its own 1697 Wilkin imprint. Use 1697, not the first part's original 1694 publication date, as this source's edition date.
- Historical components reviewed: Part I and its close; its advertisement for Astell's correspondence with John Norris; Part II's title page, dedication to Princess Ann of Denmark, introduction, four chapters, errata, and publisher's advertisement list. The dedication and introduction are part of Astell's historical work, with no modern editor or translator credited. A dedicatee is not an additional author.
- Marginal citations and quotations are already present in the 1697 text. They include John Norris's *Conduct of Human Life*, William Wotton's *Reflections*, *Art of Thinking* / *L'art de Penser*, Descartes, Locke, Boyle, Henry More, *Ladies Calling*, biblical passages and ancient examples. The final advertisements also name Patrick, Abbadie, Woodward, Pix and Filmer. These are historical quotations or book listings, not supplied modern editions of those authors. Their presence in the 1697 publication dates the retained words more than 228 years before the 1925 contributor cutoff; no modern rendering is substituted. No separate post 1697 translator, introduction, commentary or edition has been included.
- Modern source matter: credit to MFR, Wayne Hammond and Distributed Proofreaders; a short transcriber's note states that printed errata were incorporated and inconsistent punctuation, capitalization, spelling and hyphenation retained. These are transcription additions covered by Gutenberg's public-domain collection policy below. No modern critical notes are present. The original provider notices and full license remain inside the bundled source.
- The text source contains no illustration binaries. Gutenberg's image edition is used only for verifying imprint and layout. The plaintext avoids the image edition's inline page numerals.
- Normalization produces two chapters: generic Chapter 1 and `PART II:`. Actual Part II chapter headings remain within its text. Public selection reasons provide the printed part/chapter locations; no invented structural chapter labels are added.
- Editorial review preserves Astell's Christian moral purpose and distinguishes a seventeenth-century argument from a modern institutional proposal. Selected text remains unchanged, including archaic spellings and Gutenberg's underscore emphasis.

## Margaret Cavendish, The Blazing-World

- Exact provider edition: [Project Gutenberg 51783](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/51783), downloaded as [pg51783.txt](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/51783/pg51783.txt). The catalogue labels it public domain in the USA.
- The [transcribed title page](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/51783/pg51783-images.html) says A. Maxwell, M.DC.LX.VIII (1668). The [University of Pennsylvania library's historical text](https://digital.library.upenn.edu/women/newcastle/blazing/blazing.html) likewise identifies the London 1668 Maxwell edition and reproduces the same prefatory portrait verse. This is not identified as the 1666 first edition.
- Historical components reviewed: title page, caption verse accompanying the portrait, signed prefatory poem to the Duchess, author's address to the ladies, first and second narrative parts, and epilogue. No modern translator, scholarly introduction or later literary appendix is present.
- Margaret and William Cavendish are separate contributors. The poem following the portrait verse is explicitly signed William Newcastle. [Westminster Abbey's records](https://www.westminster-abbey.org/abbey-commemorations/commemorations/william-margaret-cavendish) report Margaret's death on 15 December 1673 and William's on 25 December 1676. Gutenberg and some catalogues give Margaret's death as 1674; this discrepancy does not affect the conservative screen. William's 1676 death is the later identified literary contributor date.
- The preceding portrait-caption verse is unsigned in this source. Its words are independently attested as part of the 1668 edition by the university library text; it is not a modern caption. We do not invent an attribution for it. Its historical publication predates the contributor cutoff by 257 years. The text-only source includes its words and an illustration placeholder, not an image or a modern photograph of the engraving. No artwork binary is being licensed or redistributed here.
- Modern source matter consists of Gutenberg's standard header/license, production credit to Marc D'Hooghe, and navigation/formatting. Gutenberg's policy covers supplemental transcription material. No source block claims permission under a continuing third-party copyright or inserts a modern translation.
- Normalization produces a single generic Chapter 1. This accurately leaves the whole work within one source chapter; public reasons specify first part or epilogue as appropriate. The title listings in the table of contents do not become false chapter boundaries.
- All eight excerpts are identified as fiction or framed as the author's epilogue. The microscopy and perception scenes are not presented as current science. The warning about academic disputes is placed within the story's preference for political unity. The friendship scene is described using the text's own platonic frame. The final comparison distinguishes classical history and myth.

## Source additions, delivery and verification

[Gutenberg's collection development policy](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/collection_development.html) states that its resulting ebooks are free of US copyright restrictions and that incidental additions such as transcriber notes and new cover art are permanently granted to the public domain. Its [license](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html) separately governs use of the Gutenberg name and notices. We retain the complete original source/license, attribution, original download route and exact source hashes. This review is limited to the United States; Gutenberg expressly does not provide worldwide clearance.

Local preparation uses the existing `normalize_source`, `export_feed` and `verify_feed_bundle` implementations. Each excerpt is an exact contiguous span with sentence boundaries, source offsets, stable IDs and text hash. The JSON schema passes for both reviewed feeds. Each book has eight non-overlapping excerpts of 12–130 words, with separate AI-marked context and short public editorial explanations. Selection audits retain surrounding text for review. A second local editorial pass checked all 16 excerpts and their context; it corrected the epilogue's context to distinguish history from myth. No claim of an independent human review is made.

Machine-readable hashes, IDs, checks and review findings are recorded in `review-summary.json` and the two `review-audit.json` files. Only the corresponding `.reviewed.json` files are proposed as publication bundles.
