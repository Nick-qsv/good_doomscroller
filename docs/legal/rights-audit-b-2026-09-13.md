# Edition rights audit B — 13 September 2026

This is a conservative publication screen, not a legal opinion or worldwide clearance. It reviews the second half of the 26 JSON bundles present directly in `corpus/published` at the start of this task, alphabetically: Lincoln through Seacole. The audited object is each **complete preserved edition bundle**, including embedded source bytes and normalized full text, rather than only the short quotations shown in the feed.

The requested screen requires an identified historical edition published no later than 1930 and identified copyright-relevant contributors who died no later than 1925, with no unresolved later contributions. Ancient authorship is not assigned an invented death date: the ancient Chinese base text of the Tao is separately distinguished from its nineteenth-century English translation. The cutoff is an intentionally restrictive operational rule; it is not a legal rule that guarantees unrestricted use in every country. Unknown contributors and unknown incorporated translations fail this screen. An exclusion below does **not** mean that U.S. publication was unlawful or that all versions of the underlying work are protected.

Source catalogs were checked on 13 September 2026. All thirteen Gutenberg catalogs say public domain **in the USA**. Their automatically generated book summaries were not used as edition proof. Instead, the review inspected preserved title pages, prefaces, contributor credits and relevant incorporated material, plus independent historical/library evidence where stated. Gutenberg's [copyright guidance](https://www.gutenberg.org/help/copyright.html) distinguishes translations and later contributions; its [license](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html) says local copyright laws also apply. Historical public-domain text does not make Gutenberg's trademark, license text, modern website material or an unrelated publisher's branding free of conditions.

## Individual findings

### The Papers and Writings of Abraham Lincoln, Complete

**UNCERTAIN — exclude the present bundle.** [Gutenberg 3253](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3253) identifies Abraham Lincoln (1809–1865), Joseph Hodges Choate (1832–1917), Theodore Roosevelt (1858–1919), Carl Schurz (1829–1906), and Arthur Brooks Lapsley, whose dates it does not supply. The [preserved text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3253/pg3253.txt) contains the Constitutional Edition heading, Lapsley's editorial material, the Roosevelt introduction, the Schurz essay, the Choate address and Lincoln–Douglas debate material. Neither its heading nor the repository's edition note establishes the exact print year. [Lapsley's publisher author record](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/author/26579) likewise has no life dates. This audit did not find primary evidence resolving them; no date is guessed.

The existing selection note correctly limits displayed quotations to Lincoln, but the full source still incorporates other contributions. Historical Lincoln texts could be separately re-established from a clean, identified source in a future task. That possibility does not clear this bundle.

### The Philosophy of Friedrich Nietzsche

**EXCLUDE.** The preserved title page identifies the **third edition, Boston, Luce and Company, 1913**. This is Mencken's study, not an original Nietzsche book. [Gutenberg 49316](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/49316) identifies H. L. Mencken as 1880–1956; that alone fails the cutoff. Nietzsche is identified as 1844–1900, which cannot clear Mencken's prose. The preface references Oscar Levy's English Nietzsche edition and the full source includes quoted and translated material; those subsidiary rights were not exhaustively cleared after the decisive Mencken failure. [Exact text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/49316/pg49316.txt).

### Pride and Prejudice

**EXCLUDE this edition, not Austen's underlying novel.** [Gutenberg 1342](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1342) identifies Jane Austen (1775–1817), but the actual [preserved text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1342/pg1342.txt) identifies **George Allen, London, 1894**, with a preface by **George Saintsbury** and illustrations by **Hugh Thomson**. The [University of Edinburgh's Saintsbury manuscript collection](https://archives.collections.ed.ac.uk/repositories/2/resources/348) establishes Saintsbury's death on 28 January 1933. The [British Library catalog record preserved as a PDF](https://ia803204.us.archive.org/19/items/the-scarlet-letter-...-with-illustrations-by-hugh-thomson.-british-library/The%20scarlet%20letter%20_%20...%20with%20illustrations%20by%20Hugh%20Thomson.%20-%20British%20Library.pdf) identifies Thomson as 1860–1920.

Saintsbury's preface is fully embedded, even though the existing note says only Austen's novel supplies feed selections. Removing only preface quotations from the feed does not remove the preserved preface from the published bundle. A clean Austen-only historical source can be considered separately later.

### The Problems of Philosophy

**EXCLUDE.** [Gutenberg 5827](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5827) identifies Bertrand Russell (1872–1970). The repository does not identify an exact historical print edition for its downloaded text; the work's 1912 first-publication date does not establish that identity. Russell's death year independently fails the cutoff, so subsidiary historical quotation/translation rights were not exhaustively cleared. [Exact text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5827/pg5827.txt). Do not label it universally free based on the U.S. catalog status.

### Sadhana: The Realisation of Life

**EXCLUDE.** The [actual preserved title page](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/6842/pg6842.txt) says **1916**, whereas the repository's note discusses the first English publication in 1913. Its author's preface expressly acknowledges incorporated translations by **Satish Chandra Roy**, **Ajit Kumar Chakravarti**, and **Surendra Nath Tagore**, and **Ernest Rhys** helping with revisions. This is not a verified solely self-translated edition.

[Gutenberg 6842](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6842) identifies Rabindranath Tagore as 1861–1941. A [Library of Congress record](https://www.loc.gov/resource/gdcmassbookdig.homeworld00tago/?st=grid) identifies Surendranath Tagore as 1872–1940. The [National Library of Wales's Rhys biography](https://biography.wales/pdf/s2-RHYS-ERN-1859.pdf) identifies Ernest Rhys as 1859–1946. Those dates fail the cutoff. Satish Chandra Roy and Ajit Kumar Chakravarti's death dates were not independently established from primary sources in this audit and are **unverified**, not silently deemed cleared. Professor James H. Woods is thanked for encouragement, which by itself does not identify an incorporated authorship contribution.

### The Story of My Life

**EXCLUDE.** The source heading identifies a **Special Edition containing additional chapters by Helen Keller**, with letters and supplementary educational material. The exact printing/year is not established by the repository metadata. [Gutenberg 2397](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2397) identifies all three principal credited writers: Helen Keller (1880–1968), John Albert Macy (1877–1932), and Annie/Anne Mansfield Sullivan (1866–1936). All fail the cutoff. [Exact text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2397/pg2397.txt). Keller's selected narrative cannot be cleared under this screen regardless of the status of the supplementary material.

### Talks to Teachers on Psychology; and to Students on Some of Life's Ideals

**UNCERTAIN — exclude the complete bundle.** [Gutenberg 16287](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/16287) identifies William James (1842–1910). The [title page](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16287/pg16287.txt) gives **Henry Holt, New York, 1925**, with 1899/1900 copyright dates. However, the book embeds substantial quotations, including *The Practice of the Presence of God* in an unidentified English translation (Fleming H. Revell edition), Lotze's *Microcosmus* in English translation, and translated *War and Peace* passages citing a Paris 1884 French edition. Translator identities are not supplied sufficiently to pass this screen.

The source also incorporates passages from Stevenson, Royce, Wordsworth, Jefferies, Whitman, Hudson and Wyckoff, among others. This audit does not claim that every incorporated contribution has been cleared. Annie Payson Call is discussed and advice attributed to her; her publisher [identifies her dates as 1853–1940](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/32421), but discussion alone is not treated as proof of an independently protected written contribution. Unresolved translations are the decisive reason for exclusion. Clean James-only excerpts are possible future research, not a clearance of this full file.

### The Tao Teh King, or the Tao and its Characteristics

**KEEP CANDIDATE under the operational screen.** [Gutenberg 216](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/216) explicitly identifies **James Legge (1815–1897)** as translator. A [New College, Oxford publication](https://www.new.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2018-12/10NCN3%20%282018%29%20Kornicki%20on%20First%20Chinese%20Book_1.pdf) independently identifies the same dates. The [1891 physical edition from Princeton Theological Seminary Library](https://archive.org/details/sacredbooksofchi01oxfo), *The Sacred Books of China: The Texts of Tâoism*, Part I, **Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1891**, identifies Legge and contains the Tao translation. Its [title-page and chapter OCR](https://archive.org/stream/sacredbooksofchi01oxfo/sacredbooksofchi01oxfo_djvu.txt) provide primary edition evidence; the chapter-one opening agrees with the preserved PG translation. This is an edition-identification check, not a claim of a fresh character-by-character collation of all 81 chapters against page images.

The [downloaded PG text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt) comprises the two parts/81 translated chapters, without the larger volume's later books, scholarly introduction or commentary. Its substantive English prose is explicitly Legge's translation. The Chinese base text is ancient and traditionally attributed to Laozi; no historically certain birth/death year is asserted. Preserve the translator credit. The modern Gutenberg notices are a separately licensed distribution wrapper, not a post-1925 translator contribution. No specific adverse rights claim was found; no universal copyright, moral-rights, trademark or local-law clearance is asserted.

### The Prophet

**EXCLUDE.** [Gutenberg 58585](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/58585) identifies Kahlil Gibran (1883–1931), which fails the cutoff. The [preserved heading](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/58585/pg58585.txt) says Knopf, New York, 1923, but the full front matter also lists *Nymphs of the Valley*, 1948, so the source as a whole cannot confidently be described as exclusively a 1923 printing. It contains an attributed Claude Bragdon quotation. Bragdon's death date and that quotation's publication history were not separately verified because Gibran already fails. No third-party illustration is claimed: the heading attributes the twelve original drawings to Gibran himself.

### The Souls of Black Folk

**EXCLUDE.** [Gutenberg 408](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/408) identifies W. E. B. Du Bois (1868–1963), which fails the cutoff. The preserved source is [the illustrated EPUB](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/408/pg408-images-3.epub), not a plain-text 1903 print facsimile. Its ZIP signature and image assets were confirmed locally. The work's first publication in 1903 and the catalog's U.S. status do not clear it under this death-year policy. Incorporated poetry, music and any separate image contributions were not exhaustively cleared after the decisive author failure.

### Theodore Roosevelt: An Autobiography

**UNCERTAIN — exclude the complete bundle.** [Gutenberg 3335](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3335) identifies Theodore Roosevelt (1858–1919). The [preserved preparer's note](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3335/pg3335.txt) identifies **Charles Scribner's Sons, 1920**, and first publication in 1913. Nevertheless, it includes third-party correspondence, including a substantial response dated **21 January 1913**, signed only with dashes, following Roosevelt's letter about marriage and the book *Mother*. The correspondent's identity and death date remain unknown. Other correspondence is also embedded, so this audit does not claim all additional contributors have been enumerated and cleared.

The existing editorial note excludes others' letters from the fifteen feed selections, but preserves them in the complete source. Roosevelt's own dated writings could be separately verified from a clean source; do not keep this full file on that basis.

### Twenty Years at Hull House; with Autobiographical Notes

**EXCLUDE.** [Gutenberg 1325](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1325) identifies Jane Addams (1860–1935), which fails the cutoff. Its [source text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1325/pg1325.txt) also identifies illustrator **Norah Hamilton**, photographer **Lewis W. Hine**, and artist **Alice Kellogg Tyler** in its lists of plates/illustrations. Their contribution dates and death dates were not independently verified in this audit because the author already fails. The source's production notes refer to a 1998 HTML conversion; the repository's 1910 first-publication statement does not identify an exact historical printing of every included item.

### Wonderful Adventures of Mrs. Seacole in Many Lands

**UNCERTAIN — exclude the complete bundle.** The [preserved title page](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/23031/pg23031.txt) identifies **London, James Blackwood, 1857**, edited by **W. J. S.**, with an introductory preface by **W. H. Russell**. [Gutenberg 23031](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/23031) identifies Mary Seacole (1805–1881), William Howard Russell (1820–1907), and the editor only by those initials. No primary evidence establishing W. J. S.'s identity/death was found in this audit. The editor may have contributed to the narrative itself; excluding the preface from selected quotations does not resolve that uncertainty. Do not invent an identity from matching initials.
