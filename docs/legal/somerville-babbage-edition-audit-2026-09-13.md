## Mary Somerville — approved source for eight prose selections

- Canonical catalogue: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/52869 . It names Mary Somerville (1780–1872), labels the ebook public domain in the USA, and credits Sonya Schermann and the Distributed Proofreading team for transcription from Internet Archive scans.
- Exact preserved download: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/52869/pg52869-images.html . The transcribed title page identifies **ninth edition, completely revised**, **London: John Murray, Albemarle Street,1858**. Printing is attributed to W. Clowes and Sons. The dedication is Somerville’s, Florence,1November1858. There is no credited later editor, translator, introduction, or appendix by a modern scholar.
- The bundle is this one XHTML file. It contains references to historic image files and plain captions, but **no image bytes, no bundled portrait, no plates, and no cover image**. No linked image was downloaded for publication. The J. Cooper portrait engraving and historical diagrams therefore are not reproduced as artwork. Their short labels and factual figure references remain in the exact source. This is not an EPUB or zipped HTML image package.
- The full retained file includes the original introduction,37sections, Somerville’s explanatory notes and index, Gutenberg’s front/end transcription notices, and its complete provider license. Transcription notices describe encoding, punctuation repair, spelling/index corrections, and a list of factual corrections; they do not contain an added interpretive essay. Provider notices are retained under the existing Gutenberg redistribution workflow.
- Content and reference inspection found historical scientific quotations in Somerville’s own1858 text, rather than a later anthology. Relevant named prose contributors include Francis Bacon (d1626), James Mackintosh (d1832), John Playfair (d1819), Louis Poinsot (d1859), Pierre-Simon Laplace (d1827), William Hyde Wollaston (d1828), John Frederick William Herschel (d1871), Charles Wheatstone (d1875), Godfrey Charles Mundy (d1860), David Brewster (d1868), Charles Babbage (d1871), William Robert Grove (d1896), Michael Faraday (d1867), Isaac Newton (d1727), Edmond Becquerel (d1891), and William Parsons, third Earl of Rosse (d1867). The text also quotes older verse/scripture and historical inscriptions. These appear inside the1858 edition; no recent translation is imported.
- The potentially ambiguous **“Mr. Power”** in SectionXXII was traced to **Joseph Power**, author of the1854 Royal Society paper on the reciprocal action of solar rays. Cambridge’s archive identifies him as1798–1868. The neighboring discussion concerns John William Draper (1811–1882), not his son Daniel (1841–1931). The Königsberg physicist Moser is **Ludwig Ferdinand Moser (1805–1880)**, not the unrelated1879–1930chemist. Stokes (1819–1903) and the other nineteenth-century scientific sources remain historical references; none is a modern editorial contributor. This matters because the preserved bundle contains more than the selected sentences.
- Somerville’s empirical argument is accompanied by natural theology. Several physical theories and numerical claims in the book are superseded (instantaneous gravitation, ether, immutable daily rotation, etc.). Selections avoid those claims or supply explicit historical context. The excerpts never imply contemporary scientific endorsement of the entire book.

### Import and editorial checks

The plain-text normalizer recognized an INTRODUCTION heading in the table of contents and assigned nearly the entire body to it. That draft was not curated or recommended for publication. The approved XHTML preserves one honest whole-book chapter label from the source rather than inventing section headings. Each public selection reason supplies its actual printed introduction/section/page location.

Eight selected spans: Introduction p1, p2, p3 (two distinct spans); SectionVIII p60; SectionXVI p130 and p134; SectionXXVII p271. The source page markers were inspected around these spans. All excerpts avoid embedded page digits, transcription notices, equations, image descriptions, quotations attributed to someone else, and incomplete opening/closing quotation marks. All eight preserve exact normalized source text; no silent quote cleanup was performed. Selection6 is124words; the rest are31–92words. Each has a short public editorial reason and separate explicitly AI-generated context.

The corresponding `.review-audit.json` contains offsets, sentence IDs, hashes, and surrounding context. JSON Schema validation and `verify_feed_bundle` both passed. Independent integration review checked all eight excerpts, surrounding context, separate AI context and public reasons. Schema and original-source reconstruction passed before packaging.

## Charles Babbage — held, exact printed edition unresolved

Downloaded source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4238/pg4238.txt . Canonical page: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/4238 . Gutenberg labels its electronic text public domain in the USA and names Charles Babbage1791–1871. The file credits Charles Aldarondo for transcription, gives an1832heading, and includes prefaces dated8June1832and22November1832(second edition).

That is insufficient to describe the full preserved file as an exact1832second edition. Inspection found:

1. The comparative plate-glass table has an **1835** column and a second table explicitly comparing1825and1835prices (plain-text lines4890and4936).
2. ChapterXX’s note3 begins **“Since the publication of the second edition of this work”** (line6139), then discusses a completed portion of Babbage’s engine.
3. ChapterXXIX includes a financial table with an1833row (line8564).

Thus the first-publication date and old preface cannot establish the age and contents of the complete transcription. These are positive edition inconsistencies, not a finding that Babbage’s original work is copyrighted. The present file may incorporate later Babbage revisions, but a reliable exact-edition comparison is still needed. No `reviewed.json`, approval tuple, or publication copy was produced for PG4238.

The acknowledged Dr. Fitton is William Henry Fitton,1780–1861; the [Royal Astronomical Society obituary index](https://www.women.ras.ac.uk/obituaries/William_Henry/Fitton) records his death13May1861and links his contemporary obituary. That resolves the named historical editorial contribution, but not the edition conflict. A library comparator is the [Smithsonian’s1832[1833]third-edition scan](https://library.si.edu/digital-library/book/oneconomyofmac00babb); another is the [Duke scan catalogued as1832second enlarged edition](https://archive.org/details/oneconomyofmachi00babb). Those alternatives were not imported or claimed to be PG4238’s source.
