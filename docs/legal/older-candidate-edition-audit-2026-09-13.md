# Older candidate edition audit — 13 September 2026

Decision: publish eight passages each from the three Gutenberg editions below (24 total). Hold the other three candidates. This is an assistant publication review under the repository's conservative screen, not legal advice or worldwide clearance. US public-domain status is the jurisdictional basis. Historical editions, later contributions, and the complete downloadable text bundle were considered; an old author's name alone does not approve an edition.

## Approved exact editions

- **Common Sense — Thomas Paine (died 1809), Bradford, Philadelphia, 14 February 1776; PG147.** The supplied title page and transcriber's notes identify this edition. The complete plain-text file includes the appendix, address to Quakers, a James Thomson (1700–1748) epigraph, and a modern transcriber-added “On Common Sense” section quoting Jefferson, Cresswell, Adams, Rush, and Lee (all died by 1826). Those endorsements are not treated as Paine passages. Gutenberg's supplements policy covers the transcriber's own additions; its catalogue identifies the entire ebook as public domain in the USA. Sources: [catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/147), [full transcription and notes](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/147/pg147-images.html), [Thomson biography](https://rpo.library.utoronto.ca/poets/thomson-james), [LOC historical edition](https://www.loc.gov/item/18023774/).
- **The Theory of Moral Sentiments — Adam Smith (died 1790), Dublin, J. Beatty and C. Jackson, 1777; PG67363.** Its title page calls it the sixth edition; this is not the London 1790 sixth edition and is not presented as the 1759 first edition originally proposed. Includes Smith's dissertation on languages and historical literary quotations. No later scholarly editor or translator is named. The transcriber describes typo corrections, retained uncertain spellings, and italics marked with underscores. The published bundle uses Gutenberg XHTML. Its whole-document locator avoids the plain-text parser mistaking table-of-contents headings for chapter boundaries. The selected spans match this transcription, not a claimed facsimile; the public reasons identify the printed part/section/chapter. XHTML references external images but does not embed their bytes; the cover notice explicitly dedicates it to the public domain. Sources: [catalogue and original publication](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/67363), [1777 bibliographic record](https://www.grubstreetproject.net/publications/N45628/).
- **The Interesting Narrative — Olaudah Equiano (died 1797), author-published London narrative, 1789; PG15399.** The text retains the Union Street author imprint, dedication dated 24 March 1789, subscribers, two-volume narrative, historical letters and footnotes. The dated dedication is evidence of the historical narrative, not independent proof of the impression used by the digitizer; no numbered first-impression claim is made. The complete ebook is listed public domain in the USA. No modern scholarly introduction or translation was found in the supplied text. Its embedded historical material includes Benezet, Clarkson, and passages from the 1773 *Dying Negro* by Thomas Day and John Bicknell; these are eighteenth-century contributions, not modern additions. Only Equiano's narrative prose is selected. The text file has a portrait caption, not portrait image bytes. Sources: [catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15399), [full text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/15399/pg15399.txt), [historical edition record](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupid?key=olbp16686), [scholarship identifying the embedded poem](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/verse-and-voice-in-equianos-iinteresting-narrativei/).

[Gutenberg's collection policy](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/collection_development.html) distinguishes digitization from authorship and dedicates incidental supplements permanently to the public domain. Original files and full provider notices remain bundled. [Gutenberg's permission guidance](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/permission.html) does not offer clearance outside the United States. Incidental historical quotations have not received an exhaustive worldwide jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction audit.
