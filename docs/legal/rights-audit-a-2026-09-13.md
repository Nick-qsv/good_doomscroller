# Exact-edition rights audit A

Research date: September 13, 2026. Scope: the first 13 filenames in the alphabetically sorted set of 26 `corpus/published/*.json` files present when this review began. This is a conservative editorial screening record, not an opinion establishing worldwide legal clearance.

## Decision rule and evidence scope

The screen requires an identified underlying print edition published no later than 1930, with its identified substantive authors, translators, and editors deceased no later than 1925. An unknown material contributor or unresolved edition provenance fails the screen. The extra death-year buffer is an internal restriction, not a representation that every country's law is a simple life-plus-100 rule. Unknown or excluded editions should leave the publicly served corpus until satisfactory replacement evidence is recorded. Exclusion does not mean the work is necessarily infringing in the United States.

I read the bundle metadata, edition notes, and preserved source front matter and checked first-party Project Gutenberg catalogue/source records online. Catalogue creator fields are used for the credited contributor names and death years. I inspected modern cover files in the two potentially retained EPUB bundles. I considered the whole preserved source, including editorial matter, because excluding an editor's introduction from selected quotations does not remove it from a publicly downloadable verification bundle. This audit does not authenticate every historical assertion or establish the authorship of each incidental quotation.

Seven editions are KEEP CANDIDATE, three are EXCLUDE, and three are UNCERTAIN with a recommendation to exclude. A KEEP CANDIDATE decision also depends on satisfying the common Gutenberg reuse conditions below.

## Edition findings

### A Vindication of the Rights of Woman

The [PG 3420 catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3420) records Wollstonecraft, 1759–1797, and a U.S. public-domain classification. The preserved [exact text source](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3420/pg3420.txt) includes *A Brief Sketch of the Life of Mary Wollstonecraft* but does not give its author or a print publisher/year. The 1792 date identifies the underlying treatise, not necessarily this entire composite edition. The corpus notes correctly say the selected passages omit the biographical/editorial material, but preserve that material in the source bundle.

**Blocker:** exact-edition provenance and the sketch's authorship/publication evidence are incomplete. Recommend excluding PG 3420 pending a traceable early edition or a documented edition-specific resolution. Do not relabel the whole bundle as a proved 1792 edition.

### Autobiography of Andrew Carnegie

The [PG 17976 catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/17976) identifies Andrew Carnegie, 1835–1919, and editor John C. Van Dyke, 1856–1932. Its [text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/17976/pg17976.txt) identifies Constable, London, 1920, and contains original prefatory prose by Louise Whitfield Carnegie, who is also the named 1920 copyright claimant. Her death in 1946 is recorded in the contemporary [September 1946 *Carnegie Alumnus*, page 8](https://iiif.library.cmu.edu/file/ALU_1946_032_001_09001946/ALU_1946_032_001_09001946.pdf).

**Blocker:** both Van Dyke and Louise Carnegie fail the death-year screen. The preface and editorial matter remain embodied in the preserved source even though the feed passages are Carnegie's narrative. Exclude this edition. The old copyright notice is historical evidence; it is not, by itself, proof of current U.S. copyright.

### The Autobiography of Benjamin Franklin

The [PG 148 catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/148) names Franklin, 1706–1790, and editor Charles William Eliot, 1834–1926. The [preserved text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/148/pg148.txt) identifies the *Harvard Classics*, P. F. Collier & Son Company, New York, 1909, with introduction and notes. This is not an unedited eighteenth-century source merely because Franklin wrote the autobiography then.

**Blocker:** Eliot died in 1926, one year beyond the chosen conservative cutoff. His introduction/notes remain in the bundle. Exclude this exact edition. An independently traced earlier edition might be suitable later.

### The Autobiography of Charles Darwin

The [PG 2010 catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2010) identifies Charles Darwin, 1809–1882, and editor Francis Darwin, 1848–1925. The [source](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2010/pg2010-images.html) explicitly identifies Francis as editor and begins with his explanatory introduction about his father's autobiographical recollections. It is the earlier family-edited version. Darwin Online's [bibliographical account](https://darwin-online.org.uk/EditorialIntroductions/Freeman_LifeandLettersandAutobiography.html) distinguishes the autobiographical chapter of the 1887 *Life and Letters* from later presentations; it supplies the print-publication context missing from the electronic title metadata alone.

**Finding:** the identified 1887 editorial layer and both credited contributors pass. Keep as a candidate specifically for the Francis Darwin version. Do not substitute the 1958 restored Nora Barlow edition or import its modern notes under this clearance record. The book metadata's electronic-update date is not a new claim that the literary edition was published in 2022.

### The Autobiography of Goethe

The [PG 52654 catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/52654) identifies Goethe, 1749–1832, translators Alexander James William Morrison, 1806–1865, and John Oxenford, 1812–1877. The [source title and advertisement](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/52654/pg52654-images.html) identify the 1897 revised edition and acknowledge incorporating renderings from a preceding American translation without identifying that predecessor in the advertisement.

**Blocker:** the named principal contributors pass, but the revision and expressly borrowed translation require further provenance. The 1897 imprint alone does not establish who supplied all changes. Recommend excluding this bundle while that gap remains. This decision is intentionally conservative and does not assert that the translation remains protected in the U.S.

### The Chemical History of a Candle

The [PG 14474 catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/14474) identifies Michael Faraday, 1791–1867, and editor William Crookes, 1832–1919. The [source title page](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/14474/pg14474-images.html) identifies a new impression, Chatto & Windus, London, 1908; the preface is signed W. Crookes. Both contributors pass.

The actual preserved EPUB contains one image, a modern generated Gutenberg cover with title, author/editor names, and a geometric background. It is not evidence of a new twentieth- or twenty-first-century literary editor. Gutenberg's [collection-development policy](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/collection_development.html) expressly places its supplemental material, including new covers and transcriber additions, permanently in the public domain. That first-party policy is the basis for accepting this incidental addition; the contributor death-year screen applies to the underlying literary edition. The EPUB's original notices remain subject to the common trademark/reuse conditions below.

**Finding:** keep candidate. Future introduction, illustration, or edition changes need their own review.

### Essays by Ralph Waldo Emerson

The [PG 16643 catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/16643) identifies Emerson, 1803–1882, and Edna Henry Lee Turpin, 1867–1952. The corpus source is the 1907 Merrill's English Texts edition, explicitly selected and edited with introduction and notes by Turpin; the full EPUB preserves that material, not only Emerson's essays.

**Blocker:** Turpin's substantive original contribution fails the death-year screen. Exclude this edition. Selecting only Emerson's words in the feed does not cure the public distribution of the full edited source in its verification bundle.

### Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus (1818)

The [PG 41445 catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/41445) identifies Mary Wollstonecraft Shelley, 1797–1851, and the Lackington, Hughes, Harding, Mavor, & Jones 1818 publication. The [source transcriber's note and title matter](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41445/pg41445-images.html) expressly identify a photo-reprint of the 1818 edition, distinct from the 1831 revision. Percy Bysshe Shelley's death in 1822 is independently corroborated by [Gutenberg's author catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/authors/search/?query=Percy+Bysshe+Shelley); including his possible preface and poem contributions does not threaten the cutoff.

The preserved EPUB has the three historical title-page images and a cover assembled from the historical title typography over a patterned background. The PG supplemental-art policy cited above supports treating its new cover/transcriber matter as a public-domain donation. No modern scholarly introduction is present in the examined title matter. The feed notes exclude preface, epigraphs, and borrowed verse from selected quotations.

**Finding:** keep candidate for this 1818 text and PG file only. This does not cover modern films, artwork, adaptations, restored editions, or translations using the same title.

### Incidents in the Life of a Slave Girl, Written by Herself

The [PG 11030 catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11030) identifies Harriet Ann Jacobs, 1813–1897, and editor Lydia Maria Child, 1802–1880. The [source title matter](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11030/pg11030-images.html) identifies the Boston 1861 publication for the author and names Child. The narrative's Linda Brent name is a pseudonym, not a different modern translator. Both identified contributors pass; the editor's original introduction does not create a late-death contributor issue.

**Finding:** keep candidate, specifically this historical edited English edition. The verification text preserves the historical contents and their notices; modern scholarship about the book is not included in this finding.

### The Letters of Charles Dickens, Vol. I, 1833–1856

The [PG 25852 catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/25852) identifies Charles Dickens, 1812–1870, Mamie Dickens, 1838–1896, and Georgina Hogarth, 1827–1917. The [source title page](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/25852/pg25852-images.html) identifies Chapman and Hall, London, 1880, edited by his sister-in-law and eldest daughter. All three credited literary contributors pass. The original reservation of translation rights is not a modern license grant or a present-day finding of subsisting copyright.

**Finding:** keep candidate for this 1880 publication. It is not clearance for later archival editions containing newly discovered letters or modern annotation. The print publication after Dickens's death is specifically recorded rather than treating the dates of the letters as publication dates.

### Life of Mozart

The [PG 67828 catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/67828) identifies Ludwig Nohl, 1831–1885, John Joseph Lalor, died 1899, and Jansen, McClurg & Company, Chicago, 1880. The [source translator's note](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/67828/pg67828-images.html) acknowledges A. W. Dohn's comparison of the entire English translation with the German original. That is a material contributor to investigate rather than silently overlooking him.

George P. Upton's contemporary reminiscence in [*Theodore Thomas: A Musical Autobiography*, volume I, printed page 117](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ac/Theodore_Thomas%2C_a_musical_autobiography%3B_%28IA_theodorethomasmu01thomiala%29.pdf) identifies the Chicago musical scholar Adolph W. Dohn and records his death in February 1901. Matching the initials, city, period, and musical/linguistic role supports the identification; that identity matching is an inference, not a civil-record authentication. Mozart, whose historical letters occur in the account, died in 1791, as recorded in the book's PG catalogue subject field.

**Finding:** keep candidate. The translator and comparator both predate the cutoff, not merely Nohl and Mozart. The retained source is plain text; its illustration placeholders do not distribute the corresponding portrait image. Historical accuracy is a separate question from quotation or copyright verification.

### Meditations / Thoughts of Marcus Aurelius Antoninus

The [PG 15877 catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15877) identifies Marcus Aurelius, 121–180, and translator George Long, 1800–1879. The [source](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/15877/pg15877-images.html) contains the Long translation, introductory biographical/philosophical essays, footnotes, and indexes. Its exposed title matter and the preserved text do not identify a print publisher and year. The known age of Long's translation does not independently identify the precise printing from which this electronic version was transcribed.

**Blocker:** insufficient exact-print-edition evidence for the deliberately strict current screen. Recommend exclusion pending a traceable early printing or a satisfactory source record. This is not a finding that Long's translation is still copyrighted; it is a documentation failure. No modern translation is covered by Long's dates.

### Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass, an American Slave

The [PG 23 catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/23) identifies Douglass, 1818–1895. The [source title](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/23/pg23-images.html) identifies the Anti-Slavery Office, Boston, 1845. Its preface and prefatory letter are by William Lloyd Garrison and Wendell Phillips. The Library of Congress corroborates [Garrison's 1805–1879 dates](https://www.loc.gov/item/11014852/) and [Phillips's 1811–1884 dates](https://findingaids.loc.gov/agents/people/38335). Thus the original prefatory material preserved in the bundle has been considered, not only the author of the selected narrative passages.

**Finding:** keep candidate for the identified 1845 publication. The modern electronic commemoration/transcriber note falls under the PG supplemental-material policy and is not an unreviewed modern scholarly introduction.

## Common reuse conditions and territorial limits

All thirteen source catalogue records expressly classify their editions as public domain in the United States. None supplies universal territorial clearance. Gutenberg's [terms of use](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/terms_of_use.html) expressly decline assurance outside the United States. The site's [copyright guidance](https://www.gutenberg.org/help/copyright) concerns its U.S. evaluation process. The internal age screen does not prove the absence of every special national right, moral right, restoration rule, wartime extension, or other non-copyright restriction.

The [Project Gutenberg license](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html), especially §§1.C and 1.E.1–1.E.7, matters separately from the age of a book. If the Gutenberg name remains associated with publicly displayed material, its prescribed standard notice must be prominent and the full license immediately accessible. This applies to parts of a work as well as full copies. Keeping a license hidden only inside base64 source is not the same as prominently displaying the required notice. The license also addresses original-format access. The application should keep the complete source notices intact and expose the notice/license/original download appropriately. Do not imply Gutenberg sponsorship or endorsement. Charging for access would require a fresh review of the commercial-use conditions.

Gutenberg's [collection-development policy](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/collection_development.html) distinguishes mechanical digitization from substantive authorship and states that supplemental transcriber material and new cover art are donated to the public domain. That is the cited basis for accepting modern incidental PG changes in the candidates. It does not rescue a twentieth-century editor's historical introduction, translation, or other substantive contribution from the contributor screen. The policy also acknowledges legacy exceptions, so each candidate's own catalogue classification remains part of the record.
